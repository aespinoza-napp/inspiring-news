\subsection{Writing Models}

\paragraph{The writer.}
\label{subsec:results-writing-trial}
The verifier, \texttt{llama3.2:3b}, was measured unfit to write: no
citation in four drafts, and an unverified claim stated as fact in three
(Section~\ref{sec:dev-writing}). Two openly licensed candidates were
tried on 19~October with the same sample article, one draft in Catalan
and one in Spanish each, on a processor with no graphics card, after
the evaluation runs had finished (Table~\ref{tab:writer-trial}).

Both candidates fix what ruled the 3B model out. Read by a person, their
Spanish was good, and their Catalan decided between them: the 4B model's
had slips a reader notices; the 9B model wrote ``litigi'' (a lawsuit)
for \emph{liti} (lithium) throughout. The 4B model is recommended:
twice as fast on a processor, half the memory, and the better Catalan
on this sample. One article per language is a trial, not a benchmark,
and the Catalan of either needs a person's reading before it is
published, which the flow already requires.

\paragraph{The reviewer.} The draft reviewer was tried on the two seeded
drafts of Subsection~\ref{subsec:benchmark-writing}, twice each per
model (Table~\ref{tab:reviewer-trial}). Only \texttt{qwen3.5:9b} found
every planted defect --- both spelling errors, the contradicted figure,
the claim judged \texttt{FALSE} --- and every one of its answers could
be read. The 3B model found no spelling error in four reviews and
scored the hallucination metric on an inverted scale; the 4B model was
good when it answered, but one of its reviews was two answers that were
not JSON at all. The 9B model still invents a little (twice, that the
Catalan subtitle states the false claim), and its English corrections
were refused for adding a figure --- the check cannot tell a figure put
right from a new one, and keeps to the safe side. It is the recommended
reviewer: a different model from the writer, as the setting is meant,
at about three and a half minutes a review on a processor.

\paragraph{The editor.} The writing-model benchmark
(Subsection~\ref{subsec:benchmark-writing}) was run on 19~October for
the three local models, one repeat each. The 3B model answered every
text usably (100\%) and detected the planted defect in 80\% of pairs
($[72, 88]$), a defect lowering its metric by only 3 to 46 points. The
4B model's first run fell under the format gate (80\%) while detecting
93\% ($[82, 100]$) of the pairs it scored: seven of its eight failures
were answers that nested each metric inside the one before, valid JSON
with every score in it, which the corrector read at the top level only.
The reader was corrected, and the 4B model's re-run and the 9B
model's run were started; both were stopped unfinished on 19~October
and resume where they stopped.
