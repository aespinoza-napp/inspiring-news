\subsection{Verification Models}
\label{subsec:results-verifier-models}

The verifier was compared on the train half of the custom set
(Subsection~\ref{subsec:benchmark-verification}), each model alone on
the development machine's processor, the Qwen models with their
reasoning mode off. Two conditions were run: each model with the
pipeline's own search, and each model on the annotator's own reference
pages, the same evidence for every model, so that the models differ by
their reading alone (Table~\ref{tab:verifier-models}).

\paragraph{The baseline stays.} With its own search \texttt{qwen3.5:4b}
scored 14.5 points below the baseline ($[-29.0, 0.0]$); on the
annotator's pages, 20.3 points below, an interval that excludes zero.
Two runs of the baseline on the same half differ by $-2.9$ points
($[-10.1, +4.3]$; 87\% of verdicts the same, $\kappa$ 0.76), so both
gaps lie beyond the noise of the comparison. The pipeline's guardrails
changed only 6 of its 69 answers; the rest is its reading. It answers
\texttt{UNVERIFIED} when the pages do not repeat the claim and
\texttt{PARTIALLY\_TRUE} for details the claim does not make --- one
claim was marked partially true because its pages did not mention a
larger diagram the claim never mentioned. \texttt{qwen3.5:9b}, on the same pages, was as accurate as the baseline
($+1.4$ points, $[-11.6, +14.5]$) but far more careful: it left 21
claims \texttt{UNVERIFIED} and answered \texttt{FALSE} to 6 true ones,
where the baseline did to 14, so the verdicts it committed to were right
72.9\% of the time against 55.6\% ($+17.4$, $[+2.7, +32.0]$), at three
times the model's time. Its own search was not run that day. By the
rule written before the runs --- accuracy, then speed --- the baseline
remains the verifier. The rule leaves one decision open: if a wrong
verdict costs more than an honest \texttt{UNVERIFIED}, as a
fact-checker's purpose suggests, the 9B model is the better verifier,
and a run on the pipeline's own evidence is the one that would settle
it.

\paragraph{The 9B model on the pipeline's own evidence.} On
22~October the 9B model read the pages the pipeline's own search had
found for the tuning half, replayed, so that it differs from the
baseline by its reading alone. With the list verifier it was right on
50.7\% of the 67 claims both models answered, against 46.3\% ($+4.5$,
$[-10.4, +19.4]$), and on 67.4\% of the verdicts it committed to,
against 47.7\% ($+19.7$, $[+4.1, +35.6]$); $\kappa$ doubled, 0.282
against 0.141. It is the first reader that both rarely calls a true
claim false (8 times, against the baseline's 24) and still catches most
altered claims (73\%). It costs time --- a call of 90~s at the median,
and two claims that timed out at 180~s --- and coverage: it leaves about
a third of the claims \texttt{UNVERIFIED}. Asked about one source at a
time (Section~\ref{sec:results-rq2}) it kept those gains with no
timeout: 52.2\%, $+7.2$ $[-5.8, +20.3]$ against the baseline, $\kappa$
0.267, 80\% of the altered claims caught, at about two minutes a claim.
Its run on the test half was stopped at 8 of 70 claims. On this
processor the 9B model is not the default; it is the case for a
graphics card or a hosted endpoint, where its call would take seconds.

\paragraph{What retrieval costs.} The same comparison measures RQ2's
second gap on the train half: the baseline on the annotator's pages
against its own search, $+10.1$ points ($[-2.9, +24.6]$), macro-$F_1$
$+0.142$ ($[+0.002, +0.257]$). Even on those pages it answers
\texttt{FALSE} to 14 of 45 true claims: the lean the tuning loop found
is the model's. The test half's three conditions are reported under
RQ2 (Section~\ref{sec:results-rq2}).
