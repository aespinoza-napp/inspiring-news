\section{RQ2 --- Verifying the Claims}
\label{sec:results-rq2}

RQ2 asks whether the pipeline can verify the claims of the news it
retrieves, and how that can be quantified. The quantification is fixed
in Section~\ref{sec:rq-metrics}: accuracy, macro-$F_1$ and Cohen's
$\kappa$ against the gold labels, natural and synthetic claims apart,
under three evidence conditions (none, retrieved, the annotator's),
with retrieval recall and the stage each error comes from.

\paragraph{What the X-Fact pilot already shows.} The only verification
runs so far are on X-Fact (Section~\ref{sec:results-pilot}), and they
answer RQ2 for political fact-checks rather than for the system's own
domain. Four findings carry over.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The zero-shot 3B verifier is weak.} On the 64-claim
    pilot it reached 29.7\% accuracy [18.8, 40.6] and a $\kappa$ of 0.07
    whose interval includes zero; on the 165 Spanish test claims, 19.4\%
    [13.3, 25.5], below always answering the majority class.
    \item \textbf{It leans to \texttt{FALSE}.} It answered
    \texttt{FALSE} to 43 of 64 pilot claims and never
    \texttt{MISLEADING}; without evidence the same model answered
    \texttt{FALSE} to 60 of 64. The lean is the model's prior.
    \item \textbf{Retrieved evidence helps, a little.} Paired over the
    same claims, the pipeline beat the closed-book model by 10.9 accuracy
    points [3.1, 20.3] on the pilot, and by an amount indistinguishable
    from zero on the Spanish test split (3.0 points [$-3.0$, 9.1]).
    \item \textbf{Errors are both retrieval and reasoning.} Under the
    trace rule 44 of 45 wrong verdicts are reasoning errors; under the
    reference rule, 27 of the 29 it can judge are retrieval errors. The
    model misread what it was shown, and what it was shown was not what
    the fact-checker had used --- no annotator link was retrieved at any
    depth.
\end{itemize}

The pilot also showed how much of an open-retrieval score on an old
benchmark is about time rather than verification: 16 of the 20 claims
for which it can be decided cited a source newer than the claim, and
the fact-checker's own verdict page was ranked for 9 of the 24 English
claims (Section~\ref{subsec:pilot-time}). The custom set avoids the
worst of this by construction: its claims are weeks old, not years, and
its annotation rule restricts the gold label to evidence available at
publication time.

\paragraph{On the custom set: the test half.} The split was frozen on
19~October (Subsection~\ref{subsec:custom-split}) and the pipeline run on
both halves the same day: \texttt{llama3.2:3b} on a processor without a
graphics card, the corpus snapshot, every claim scored and none set
apart. Every decision --- here, whether to turn on the second look --- was
taken on the tuning half before the test half's report was read.
Table~\ref{tab:custom-test} gives the test half; its headline is the
default pipeline, the first pass.

Three findings. \textbf{On the natural claims the pipeline agrees with
the labels no better than chance} ($\kappa = -0.01$): it answered
\texttt{FALSE} to 18 of the 46 \texttt{TRUE} claims, the lean towards
\texttt{FALSE} that the X-Fact pilot showed. \textbf{It catches the
synthetic refuted claims} --- 12 of 15 --- whose evidence plainly
contradicts a substituted figure or date. And \textbf{the labels' source
matters}: on the 20 natural claims the annotator labelled the pipeline
agrees with gold on 6 (30\%), on the 35 the AI pass labelled on 19
(54\%). The samples are small and the intervals overlap, but the
direction is the one a gold set labelled by a language model invites
--- a second model agreeing with the first --- and is why the
annotator's review of those labels (Section~\ref{sec:results-rq3})
comes before any of these numbers is final.

\textbf{Retrieval} found evidence for every claim (4.9 ranked sources
from 21 candidates), but rarely the annotator's: the annotator's link
was among the candidates for 14.5\% of the claims, ranked for 8.7\% and
cited for 5.8\%; the annotator's domain among the candidates for 34.8\%,
ranked for 23.2\% and cited for 13.0\%.

\paragraph{The tuning loop and the second look.} On the tuning half
every claim whose verdict disagreed with gold --- 38 of 69 --- was
looked at again (Subsection~\ref{subsec:tuning-loop}):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{9 fixed} by the second look: a second search, planned
    from what the first verdict said was missing, found the evidence.
    \item \textbf{8 retrieval errors}: still wrong after the second look,
    but right when judged on the annotator's own pages --- the decisive
    source exists and the search never found it.
    \item \textbf{15 for a person to decide}: on the annotator's own
    pages the model gives the pipeline's verdict again. They are mostly
    the model's: a \texttt{TRUE} label judged \texttt{FALSE} (5), a
    synthetic \texttt{FALSE} judged \texttt{TRUE} on its source's
    evidence (4, the substitution unnoticed), an \texttt{UNVERIFIED}
    label judged \texttt{FALSE} (3); several explanations restate the
    claim as supported and conclude \texttt{FALSE}.
    \item 2 reasoning errors (a third verdict on the annotator's pages),
    2 claims whose pages could not be read and 2 without pages.
\end{itemize}

Had the gold label chosen which claims get a second look, accuracy would
have risen from 44.9\% to 58.0\%. The pipeline cannot use the gold
label; its own trigger --- a verdict \texttt{UNVERIFIED} or below 0.5
confidence --- fired on 15 claims and gained 2.9 points [$-2.9$,
$+8.7$], fixing 3 claims and breaking 1. That gain does not exclude
zero and costs a second look on a fifth of the claims, so the second
look was left off by default. On the test half, where the pipeline took
its second looks itself, it fired on 17 claims and changed the accuracy
by 0.0 points [$-4.3$, $+4.3$], one claim fixed and one broken: the
decision held. The loop's diagnosis says where the next points are. The
model is wrong with confidence more often than it is unsure, so a trigger
on doubt cannot find most of its errors; and on the annotator's own
evidence it still contradicts 15 labels, most of them correct. The
larger error is the 3-billion-parameter verifier's reasoning, which the
model comparison (Section~\ref{sec:results-models}) addresses, not the
search.
\paragraph{Retrieval as an ablation.} On 20~October the test half's 70
claims were searched three times in one window of fifty minutes,
stopping before the model, with no internal corpus: all three queries,
the anchor query alone, and anchor and proposition without the
refutation query. Every claim was searched; Brave and Google refused
the meta-search engine throughout and the other engines answered.
Paired against all three queries:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The anchor query alone finds less.} The annotator's
    domain was among the candidates for 14.5\% of the claims against
    33.3\% ($-18.8$ points [$-29.0$, $-10.1$]), the annotator's link for
    10.1\% against 15.9\% ($-5.8$ [$-11.6$, $-1.5$]); it brought 8
    candidates a claim instead of 19.5 and saved 5.3 seconds of retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Anchor and proposition recover nearly all of it}:
    $-4.3$ points [$-10.1$, 0.0] on the annotator's domain among the
    candidates, $+1.5$ [0.0, $+4.3$] among the ranked, 2.7 seconds a claim
    faster than all three.
\end{itemize}

So the proposition query earns its place, and the refutation query
shows no measurable gain on this set --- which has no natural
\texttt{FALSE} claim for it to surface. No verdict was asked, so whether
the refutation query changes verdicts is not measured; and the
annotator's links are a strict witness. The three variants' top five
pages, 603 of them, are pooled for relevance judgement.
\paragraph{Two of the three evidence conditions.} On 20~October the
same model judged the test half's 70 claims with no evidence at all and
on the annotator's own pages. Closed-book it scored 34.3\% [22.9, 45.7]
and answered \texttt{FALSE} to 40 of 70; on the annotator's pages,
54.3\% [42.9, 65.7], $\kappa = 0.26$ [0.09, 0.42], natural claims
52.7\%. Paired over the same claims the annotator's evidence is worth
20.0 points [5.7, 34.3]: 22 claims only the oracle got right, 8 only the
closed-book model. How much of that the pipeline's own search recovers
is the third condition. The full run made again that day did not
finish; a fresh one on 22~October (\texttt{66034c5fea72}), its verifier
reading the passages about the claim rather than each page's opening
since 21~October, scored 51.4\% [40.0, 62.9], $\kappa = 0.14$. Paired
over the same claims against the oracle it is 2.9 points lower
[$-17.1$, $+11.4$]: 14 claims only the oracle got right, 12 only the
search. Two days and that change to the reader separate the two runs,
and the closed-book run cannot be paired with it (its per-claim records
stayed on another machine), but the order is plain: on this half the
search recovers most of what the annotator's pages are worth to this
model, and what is left is the model's reading.

\paragraph{Where the errors come from.} The same run gives each of its
34 wrong verdicts the stage that made it
(Subsection~\ref{subsec:harness-attribution}). By the trace alone, 32
are reasoning errors and 2 aggregation errors: the model had ranked
evidence and answered wrongly on it. By the reference rule, 26 of the
33 it can judge are retrieval errors --- the annotator's link was not
ranked --- 5 reasoning and 2 aggregation. The two rules disagree on 26
claims, the pattern of the X-Fact pilot: the search finds pages other
than the annotator's, and the model misreads the pages it finds.

\paragraph{The verifier's \texttt{FALSE}.} That run answered
\texttt{FALSE} to 17 of the 46 \texttt{TRUE} claims, and the tuning
half showed why. Each such answer marked one source as contradicting the
claim and left the rest without a stance, and most explained themselves
by absence (``the evidence does not mention\ldots''), where the right
ones named the other value. It is not mainly the search: of the 29 true
tuning claims that carry a figure, 22 had a ranked source holding every
figure, and the model answered \texttt{FALSE} to 12 of those. Variants
of the verifier were then compared on fixed evidence, each a
\emph{replay} of one searched run's pages, after the baseline replayed
on ten claims gave the same verdicts, stances and explanations ten times
in ten: at temperature zero the 3B model is deterministic, and every
difference is the variant's. The rule, written before the runs, asked of
a variant at least the baseline's accuracy and fewer confidently wrong
verdicts, on both halves. Stated verdict definitions and a quote asked
before the stance did not meet it. One variant did: the verifier asks
about \emph{one source at a time} --- supports, contradicts, silent or
unrelated, with a quote --- and the side more outlets take wins, a tie
being \texttt{UNVERIFIED} (Table~\ref{tab:per-source}).

It stopped calling true claims false and stopped catching most claims
with one altered detail: asked whether a source states a claim, the 3B
model answers ``supports'' when the story is the same and one figure is
not. On the tuning half the same trade held (natural claims 35.2\% to
53.7\%, altered ones 80.0\% to 26.7\%). On the natural claims of the
test half, the claims the application actually checks, it is right
78.7\% of the times it commits. It has been the default since
22~October; every other result in this section is the list verifier's,
which remains a setting.
