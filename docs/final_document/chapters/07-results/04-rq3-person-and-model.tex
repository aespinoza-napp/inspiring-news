\section{RQ3 --- How Close a Model Comes to a Person}
\label{sec:results-rq3}

RQ3 compares a person and a language model taking the same decision on
the same input, in three places (Section~\ref{sec:rq-metrics}): which
candidates to publish, which label a claim deserves, and the verdict;
the annotator's agreement with their own blind re-label is the ceiling.
None of the three has yet been measured against a person, and this
section says what exists and what is missing.

\paragraph{Labelling: the AI pass against the annotator.} 108 of the
139 facts in the custom set --- 78 natural claims and the 30 synthetic
ones --- carry a label proposed by the AI labelling pass, each marked as
such in its note. The annotator's review, keeping or changing each, is
P0 work. Its outcome is the first RQ3 number: the agreement, as
Cohen's $\kappa$ with its confusion matrix, between a strong language
model applying the annotation guide and the person who wrote it. From
the review onward the annotator labels each batch before the AI does,
so that every new fact adds to that comparison without anchoring.

annotator's review, on the 108 facts, with the confusion matrix and the

the hash-chosen 20\%, as the ceiling.}

\paragraph{Selection: the AI against the editor.} The only comparison
so far is indirect: on the 6~October candidates, the AI selection's
0--10 score separated stories worth offering from the rest with an AUC
of 0.650 [0.533, 0.759], below the ranking's 0.778, but both were
measured against labels the AI labelling pass assigned, not against an
editor (Table~\ref{tab:ranking-auc}). Rounds already sent with the AI's
help are \emph{assisted}, and their agreement is inflated, because an
editor anchors on a proposal; the number to report comes from
\emph{blind} rounds, where the editor sends a choice before the model is
run.
\pending{ten blind and ten assisted rounds: precision, recall and
Jaccard of the AI's picks against the editor's, and the AUC of its score,
blind against assisted.}

\paragraph{Consistency: each against itself.} The protocol requires a
fifth of the test half to be evaluated a second time
(Subsection~\ref{subsec:custom-review}): 14 claims. The system's half of
that requirement is done. The 14 were run through the pipeline again
1~hour 51~minutes after the test run, with the same model and settings
(Table~\ref{tab:consistency}). 13 of the 14 verdicts were the same
twice --- $\kappa = 0.84$ [0.44, 1.00] --- although the evidence was
not: the two runs' ranked sources overlapped by a mean Jaccard of 0.58,
identically for 5 claims and not at all for 2. The one verdict that
changed, \texttt{FALSE} then \texttt{TRUE} for a claim labelled
\texttt{TRUE}, changed with entirely different evidence; no verdict
changed on the same evidence. Over a short interval, then, the system's
inconsistency comes from the web it searches rather than from the model,
and it is small next to its disagreement with the labels. With 14
claims the interval is wide, and two hours is not the week the
annotator's re-label waits. The annotator's half --- the blind second
label of the same 14 --- is still to be done.

on the reviewed custom set (Section~\ref{sec:results-rq2}), read against

agrees with the 20 labels the annotator set on 6 and with the 35 the AI
pass set on 19.
