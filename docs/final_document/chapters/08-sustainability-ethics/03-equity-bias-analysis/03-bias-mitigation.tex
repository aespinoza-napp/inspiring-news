\subsection{Actions Taken to Reduce Them}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Stratification.} The custom set targets six facts in
    each of 25 verdict-by-group cells, the daily batch prefers the
    groups that are short, and cells that stay short are reported with
    their real counts rather than padded.
    \item \textbf{Rules fixed before labelling.} The four tie-break rules
    were written before the first label, and those that can be checked
    are enforced by the labelling tool on save.
    \item \textbf{The blind re-label and $\kappa$.} 20\% of the facts,
    chosen by hash, are labelled again blind and at least a week later;
    agreement is measured on the first label.
    \item \textbf{AI labels disclosed and reviewed.} Every label the AI
    pass proposed says so in its record, none counts as gold until the
    annotator reviews it, and their agreement is a reported result.
    \item \textbf{Partitions reported apart.} Natural and synthetic
    claims, English and Spanish, each topic group, and the claims whose
    search or model failed are reported separately, so that a strength
    in one cannot hide a weakness in another.
    \item \textbf{Every rating says what it is.} An unrated domain is
    shown as unrated; a screened candidate is listed with the reason and
    the margin that left it out, so an editor can see what the screen
    removed.
    \item \textbf{Two languages from the start.} Every lexicon exists in
    both languages, and a source in a third language fails the test
    suite rather than being scored with English word lists --- a fix for
    a measured bias, when the same story scored 0.875 for
    constructiveness in English and 0.0 in Spanish.
\end{itemize}
