\subsection{Biases in the Data}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The source catalogue.} The 37 sources were chosen by
    hand, and their reliability ratings were set by hand. They are
    mostly European and North American outlets, plus Infobae
    (Argentina), The Conversation (Australia) and international bodies;
    voices from the rest of the Global South, local
    and community media, and outlets in other languages are absent. Only
    the configured domains have a real rating; every other domain found
    as evidence gets a neutral default, flagged as unrated, which favours
    sources the catalogue already knows when the ranking breaks ties.
    \item \textbf{Two languages.} English and Spanish only; the Spanish
    side has fewer enabled sources (13 against 20), only three in the
    environment group, and a claim selector that is markedly less
    precise in Spanish (0.52 against 0.70).
    \item \textbf{The custom set's balance.} By design it is balanced
    over five topic groups and five verdicts, but the natural claims
    contain no \texttt{FALSE} and one \texttt{MISLEADING}, two thirds of
    the facts are in English, and science, environment and health hold
    more facts than society and culture
    (Table~\ref{tab:custom-matrix}).
    \item \textbf{One annotator and an AI pass.} The labels reflect one
    person's application of the guide, and for most facts the
    proposals of one language model, whose own biases in reading
    evidence are not known.
    \item \textbf{X-Fact's domain.} The external benchmark is political
    statements checked by fact-checkers, selected because someone
    disputed them, from two organisations in the pilot.
\end{itemize}
