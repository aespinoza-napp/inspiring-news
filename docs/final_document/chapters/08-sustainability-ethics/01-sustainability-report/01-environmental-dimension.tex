\subsection{Environmental Dimension}

\paragraph{No model was trained.} The largest environmental decision of
the project is negative: every model is used as published, from open
weights. Training or fine-tuning a language model, even a small one,
costs orders of magnitude more energy than running it, and none of the
research questions needed it. The verifier is a 3-billion-parameter
model rather than an 8-billion one, chosen for its footprint.

\paragraph{The energy of running it.} The energy of the system is the
energy of the machine it runs on, for the time it runs. That time is
measured: about 36~s per claim on the development laptop, of which
about 15~s is search and page fetching, 9~s ranking and 6~s the model;
and about three minutes per four-claim article on a CPU-only server,
almost all of it the model. The power drawn was not
measured. As an order of magnitude only, at an assumed 100~W for a
laptop under load, the whole X-Fact pilot (64 claims, two at a time,
about 18 minutes of wall-clock time) cost about 30~Wh, or about half a
watt-hour per claim; the figure should be read as an estimate under
that assumption, not as a measurement. Several design choices reduce the work per article: the
admission filter rejects an article before any search or model call;
the claim selector keeps at most four claims; claims with no evidence
are not sent to the model; repeated queries are sent once; and the
selection step means only the articles a person or the model chose are
analysed at all, rather than everything discovery finds.

\paragraph{The server.} The production target is one shared virtual
machine, sized from measured memory use, rather than a set of services
each sized for a peak; it was never run, so it has consumed nothing.
Choosing a region whose electricity has a low carbon intensity is a
decision for the real deployment.
