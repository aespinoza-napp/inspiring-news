\subsection{Domain Limitation and a Custom Validation Set}
\label{subsec:custom-gold-set}

Nevertheless, X-Fact does not fully capture the nature of this
work's application domain either. Its claims originate predominantly
from political discourse and controversial public statements, while
the developed system is applied to constructive news about science,
the environment, health, culture and society, from the positive-news
outlets, general newspapers, public bodies and academic publishers of
its source catalogue (Section~\ref{subsec:scraping-sources}). Their
claims are mostly reports of studies, projects, figures and events,
often sourced to the organisation the story is about, and they sit
beside interpretations of those facts that no evidence can settle ---
a mix virtually absent from X-Fact, whose claims were chosen by
fact-checkers precisely because someone disputed them.

For this reason, the quantitative evaluation in this work relies on
\textbf{two complementary sources}: X-Fact \cite{gupta2021xfact}, as
a standardised benchmark enabling comparison of the proposed method
against the state of the art in multilingual, open-retrieval fact
verification; and a custom validation set, built from claims
extracted from real articles of the configured sources and labelled
by hand (Section~\ref{sec:custom-dataset}), designed to measure the
system on the claims of the domain it was built for --- the real use
case, and one that no existing academic benchmark covers.
