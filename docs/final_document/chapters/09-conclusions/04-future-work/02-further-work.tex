\subsection{Further Work}
\label{subsec:future-further}

\paragraph{Evaluating the writer.} The writing flow is built
(Section~\ref{sec:dev-writing}) but has been measured only on a handful
of drafts with a model shown to be unfit for it. Its evaluation would
take a larger, openly licensed writer model with a context of at least
16k tokens; extract each draft's claims with the pipeline's own
extractor and verify each against the draft's own sources only, with
no web search; and score the share of supported claims
\cite{min2023factscore} and the precision and recall of the citations
\cite{gao2023alce}, validated by a hand check. The pipeline's verifier
would become the evaluator of its own writer --- validated, not assumed.
Publishing to social networks would first need a stage that turns an
article into media.

\paragraph{Asking questions before searching.} AVeriTeC records evidence
as question--answer pairs, and its strongest systems ask questions before
they search (Section~\ref{subsec:sota-what-to-search}). The pipeline
asks none, and its stage-by-stage check found what that costs: the
decisive figure and the kind of source that would hold it are missing
from its queries. A question plan would answer both, and a question
per detail would make the verifier compare a changed figure rather than
the claim as a whole, its weakest case. It would also make the official
AVeriTeC score possible: without question--answer pairs the pipeline is
scored on its verdict alone. Against it, the literature is split. More
sub-claims add noise and help a weak verifier only up to a point
\cite{hu2025decomposition}; models find the literal questions far more
often than the implied ones \cite{chen2022claimdecomp}; and pieces cut
too small lose the context that makes them checkable
\cite{gunjal2024molecular}, which is what this work met when a claim's
subject, dengue, was named only in its headline. Here the 3B verifier
would also be the model writing the questions, and every question
multiplies the searches and model calls of a claim that already takes
about 44~s on the CPU; aggregation would need a rule as well, since the
article verdict is already worst-claim-wins. The experiment is
therefore a new query plan beside the existing ones, not a replacement:
at most three questions, each carrying the claim's subject and one
decisive detail of the rule-based checkable point, so that a question
the claim does not support is rejected as livecheck rejects a claim not
in the transcript; queries written per question; tried first on
AVeriTeC's training split, then measured on the 500 development claims
in both evidence conditions against the rule-based plan, with the
question--answer score reported beside accuracy and compared with
InFact, HerO and ClaimCheck
\cite{rothermel2024infact,yoon2024hero,putta2025claimcheck}.

\paragraph{Evidence against the age of a claim.} Re-running retrieval on
the same recent claims after a week and after a month, to put a number
on how much evidence a claim gains as it ages --- the question that
benchmarks of fresh knowledge ask of language models \cite{vu2024freshllms}
and that the custom set's Rule~1 meets every day, when a new study has
only its own press release.

\paragraph{The rest of the product.} The recommendation system over the
graph, with the verdict as one of its signals, and the mobile
application of the wider project (Section~\ref{sec:sota-selection}).

\paragraph{Operations.} The first real cloud deployment, with a search
check from a data-centre address; reading \texttt{robots.txt}; evicting
finished jobs; expiring the analysis cache; normalising URLs before
comparing them; and the graph measured at scale beyond a few thousand
articles.

\paragraph{Quality.} A frontend test runner, beyond the end-to-end
checks; a policy for the three bluffs
that still pass the guardrails; deciding publication on the found
verdict and the coverage counts rather than the worst claim, now that
the response carries both; and the topic classifier, measured on the
reviewed set and improved if it matters.
