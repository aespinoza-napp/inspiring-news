\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:answers}

The answers below are provisional: they state what the measurements
made so far support, and each ends with the measurement still needed.

\paragraph{RQ1 --- Finding.} \emph{Yes, on the evidence so far.} A round
scoped to one topic group read 0.41 times the sources and 0.10 times the
links of an every-topic reading; its ranking separated stories worth
offering from the rest with an AUC of 0.78 [0.67, 0.87]; and after the
alignment of 9~October the first twenty places of five one-group rounds
held no non-articles and 17 stories not worth offering out of 100,
against 13 and 29 before. All of it rests on labels assigned by the AI
labelling pass and on one or two days of feeds.
\pending{settle with the author's own labels on at least two rounds and
discovery on five days.}

\paragraph{RQ2 --- Verifying.} \emph{Not yet with the 3B model.} On X-Fact the pipeline reached 29.7\% accuracy
[18.8, 40.6] on the stratified pilot and 19.4\% [13.3, 25.5] on the
Spanish test split, with a $\kappa$ against gold indistinguishable from
zero; retrieved evidence added 10.9 points [3.1, 20.3] over the model
alone on the pilot and nothing measurable on the Spanish test split.
How it can be quantified is the other half of the question, and that
half is answered: by the harness's metrics with intervals, the evidence
conditions, retrieval recall and the stage attribution. On the custom
set's test half, with the default pipeline, it reached 52.9\% [41.4,
64.3]: it caught 12 of 15 synthetic refuted claims, but on the natural
claims agreed with the labels no better than chance ($\kappa = -0.01$),
calling 18 of 46 supported claims false. A second, wider search for
verdicts in doubt, tuned on the other half, changed nothing on the
test half; the tuning loop placed most errors in the 3B model's
reasoning rather than in the search. The same claims run twice gave the
same verdict 13 times in 14. The three evidence conditions place the
gap: on the test half the model alone scored 34.3\%, on the annotator's
pages 54.3\% (+20.0 points [5.7, 34.3]), and with its own search, two
days later, 51.4\% --- indistinguishable from the annotator's pages
($-2.9$ [$-17.1$, $+11.4$]). The search recovers most of what evidence
is worth to this model; the reading is the limit. Asking the model about
one source at a time, the default since 22~October, cut its
\texttt{FALSE} answers to true claims from 17 of 46 to 2 and raised
selective accuracy from 52.2\% to 66.1\% (+13.9 [0.3, 27.3]), but it
caught 2 of 15 altered claims instead of 9. A 9B model catches them and
rarely calls a true claim false, at two to three times the time on a
processor.
\pending{settle on the annotator's reviewed labels: 35 of the 55 natural
test claims carry the AI pass's label.}

\paragraph{RQ3 --- Person and model.} \emph{Not yet measured.} The
comparisons are designed and their data are being produced: 108 labels
proposed by the AI pass await the annotator's review, and the blind
selection rounds have not been run. The system's own consistency is
measured: 13 of 14 verdicts the same on a second run ($\kappa = 0.84$
[0.44, 1.00]), the one change coming from different evidence.
\pending{$\kappa$ between the AI pass and the annotator; between the AI
selection and the editor on blind rounds; between the pipeline and the
annotator; and the annotator's self-agreement as the ceiling.}

\paragraph{RQ4 --- The system.} \emph{Partly, and the person is still
needed at every step.} The system takes on the reading of the sources
and a first ordering of what it finds; about two in three of the claims
it proposes are checkable (precision 0.64 [0.57, 0.70], lower in
Spanish); and its verdicts are not yet reliable enough to be used
without checking. On the custom test half, asked one source at a time,
it commits to 84.3\% of the claims and is right on 66.1\% of those:
44.3\% [32.9, 55.7] of its verdicts would be usable without re-checking,
and 28.6\% [18.6, 38.6] are confidently wrong. A claim takes about 45
to 60~s on a desktop processor, or 14--18~s at some 17 points less
accuracy.
\pending{the same on the reviewed labels, and time per fact from the
labelling tool against time per claim.}
