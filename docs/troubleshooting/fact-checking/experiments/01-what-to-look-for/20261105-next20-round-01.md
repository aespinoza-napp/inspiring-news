## Findings

Read from `round-01/comparison-before.json`, under the base `{}` (`checkable_terms: sentence`).

1. **The six term slots go to the sentence's framing and its verbs, and the
   finding comes after them** (stage 01, `checkable.py`, `MAX_TERMS`). The
   commonest failure, 7 claims:
   fact086 (`pipeline.checkablePoint.terms`: primera autora investigadora
   resultados muestran obesidad; `counted.checkablePoint.missing`:
   hiperglucemia, remodelación molecular, macrófagos), fact088 (colleague
   associate professor orthopedic surgery discovered; missing: cartilage, older
   mice, knee), fact085 (estudio liderado identificado ...; missing: disfunción
   diastólica), fact125 (estudio participaron ...; missing: anticonceptivos
   hormonales), fact122 (main goal alter trajectory ...; missing: third),
   fact123 (flew above snapped series images onboard; missing: monitoring
   cameras), fact106 (later arrived debris laden front lawn; missing: maple,
   crew). In the first five the slots taken are words of who did, said or
   found it (study, led, author, researcher, professor, results, main goal);
   in the last two they are plain verbs, which no word list of framing covers.
2. **The subject the sentence never says** (stage 01, but not from the
   sentence): fact107 (missing: Wyandotte County), fact114 (2050), fact117
   (Movistar Arena), fact118 (ONT), fact106 (plea). Background a reader brings,
   not in the sentence; the headline lever (`pipeline.checkablePoint.headline`)
   restored fact107's "tarp" but cannot hold these. `claim_restatement` or the
   article's other sentences are the levers, not `checkable.py`.

   letters) is not.

## Proposed change

**What.** Switch on the framing-last order of the checkable point's terms:
words of who did, said or published it (`checkable.FRAMING`, en and es:
reporting and attribution, research roles and acts, discourse framing) move
after the finding's words before `terms[:MAX_TERMS]` is taken. Nothing is
dropped; only which six are kept moves. It addresses finding 1's first five
claims; the two whose slots go to plain verbs (fact106, fact123) it does not.

**Where.** `backend/src/services/fact_checker/retrieval/checkable.py`,
`checkable_point` → `framing_last`, already in the code behind
`PipelineThresholds.checkable_terms` (default `"sentence"`) and read by
`point_for_thresholds`. Built by `agent --implement` on this batch, never
through the loop: **no new code this round.**

**Lever.** `checkable_terms: "framing_last"`, alone. Different from the
earlier non-loop try (`try.json`), which set it together with `query_plan:
checkable`: this round changes only the checkable point, so the sentence plan
in use stays as the base has it, and since then the names (cardiomiocitos,
wind and solar, tarp) have moved the base from 0.732 to 0.767, so its
gains are re-measured here, not carried over.

**Mode.** Offline (the checkable point). Under the base's `query_plan:
sentence` it changes no query; it reaches a search only with `query_plan:
checkable`, a later round or a searched try.

**Prediction** (from reading the code against `pipeline.checkablePoint.terms`;
the figures are the script's to measure):
- fact086 gains hiperglucemia, remodelación molecular (0.5 → about 0.833);
- fact088 gains older mice (0.5 → 0.667; cartilage, knee stay past the sixth slot);
- fact122 gains third (0.5 → 0.667);
- fact125 gains anticonceptivos hormonales (0.75 → 1.0), so
  `checkablePointAllCarried` 5 → 6;
- fact085 unchanged ("disfunción" enters, "diastólica" stays seventh);
- the other 15 unchanged. No tune claim can lose: only framing words move
  back, and none of Claude's decisive details in this batch is one.
  `checkablePointCoverage` up from 0.767, `sentencePlanCoverage` unchanged.

**What could get worse.** A claim whose point is its framing (who led a
study, that someone is a professor, what a report revealed) loses those words
from the terms when the sentence has more than six; on the held-out batch
that is the one way it can fall. The word list was written reading train
claims: it holds word classes, not these claims' words, but it may still fit
them. Default `"sentence"` keeps every earlier run reproducible.
