4. Verification: the stage the measurements say limits you

Paper: MiniCheck (Tang, Laban, Durrett, EMNLP 2024) — arXiv · code
How it helps you: New, and the most useful. It asks whether a document supports a claim, which is your per-source question. A 770M Flan-T5
reaches GPT-4 accuracy at about 400× lower cost. On your CPU that could take the model out of the per-source loop, the same goal as
Laya's planned source-stance decision (D4). Test it by replay against V2b on the same evidence; it's open and runs locally. It also
reports that splitting claims into parts didn't consistently help, which matches your results.
────────────────────────────────────────
Paper: QuanTemp (Venktesh et al., SIGIR 2024) — arXiv · QuanTemp++ (2025) — arXiv
How it helps you: New. Real numerical claims: comparisons, statistics, intervals, dates. They find claim decomposition and number-aware
models help. That's your open weakness, the altered detail (caught 60% → 13%). It's a ready test set for "compare the figures, dates and
names one by one", and QuanTemp++ also corrects temporal leakage.
────────────────────────────────────────
Paper: Quelle & Bovet, The Perils & Promises of Fact-checking with LLMs (Frontiers in AI, 2024) — link
How it helps you: New. LLM fact-checkers with retrieval, whose accuracy shifts with the claim's language and with whether it's true or
false. That matches your 3B's lean towards FALSE and your English/Spanish gap. Good for the discussion and threats to validity.
────────────────────────────────────────
Paper: Schuster et al., Towards Debiasing Fact Verification Models (EMNLP 2019) — arXiv (already cited)
How it helps you: Your 30 synthetic FALSE claims carry the same risk it found in FEVER: the claim text alone gives the label away. A
claim-only model reached 61.7%. Check that your closed-book run doesn't do well on the altered claims for that reason; it scored 60% on
them.
