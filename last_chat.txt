2. Fixing the distribution.

Of the 109 real claims, none was FALSE. A system that always says TRUE would have scored 83%. I added 30 synthetic FALSE claims (fact149 to fact178), the method FEVER and SciFact used:
- Each takes one verified TRUE claim and changes the thing it asserts: a number, a date, a name or a relation. It keeps the source's article and evidence, so that evidence is what refutes it.
- There are 6 per topic group, 9 of them in Spanish.
- No negations. Adding "not" let a model guess FEVER's refuted claims without reading any evidence (Schuster et al. 2019).
- Only what the claim asserts, never a detail. Under your Rule 2, a wrong detail makes a claim PARTIALLY_TRUE, not FALSE.
- Reported apart. Every evaluation report now shows real and synthetic claims separately, as the papers do.
- Labeller support. It records which fact each mutation came from, refuses bad sources, keeps that link when you edit a mutation in the form, and lists mutations whose source you later relabel. "python labeller/app.py mutate" creates new ones.

The set is now 139 facts: 91 TRUE, 30 FALSE, 10 UNVERIFIED, 7 PARTIALLY_TRUE, 1 MISLEADING.
