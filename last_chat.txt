1. Docker rebuilds.

Cause: Docker Desktop keeps at most 20 GB of build cache, and it held 21 GB. Rebuilding one image evicted the other's 2.7 GB dependency layer, so a rebuild with no changes took 291 s.
Fix: the limit is raised to 60 GB in Docker Desktop's settings file (C:\Users\Adria Espinoza G\.docker\daemon.json). It takes effect when Docker Desktop restarts. uv's download cache is now kept between builds, so a dependency change downloads only what changed.
Result: a rebuild after a code change takes about 30 s, and one with no changes about 2 s.

2. Discovery aligned with the mission and the groups.

Method: five one-group rounds were run live, then re-run after each change, and 318 candidates were labelled by Claude from title, summary and link (not yet checked by a person).
Result, first twenty of all five rounds (100 candidates), before and after:
- not an article: 13 -> 0
- belongs to another group: 21 -> 13
- not worth offering: 29 -> 17
- clearly positive: 34 -> 34

Health improved most: not worth offering 10 -> 3, clearly positive 3 -> 4.

An hour later, on the 16:35 feeds, the result held: 0 not articles, 13 from another group, 16 not worth offering, 33 clearly positive. Only 6 of the 100 were new, so it is a weak test. The real one is the next days' rounds, labelled before the rules see them.

What changed:
- Pages that are not articles are left out: podcasts, roundups, quizzes, galleries, media advisories, promotions, shopping, branded content, recipes and more live pages. 28 of the 35 such pages were caught, and no article.
- Candidates nearer another group's topics are listed after those that fit, never dropped, and a source's three slots go first to what fits. This told the groups apart with AUC 0.87.
- Markets, service pages, strikes and protests, disasters and celebrity are now left out at margin 0.02 instead of 0.04: in that band, 15 of 16 were not worth offering. Crime and war stay at 0.04.
- English topic keywords now match whole words. "ai" had matched "said", and "who" matched every "who": 85 of 419 matches were false.
- Section-page links now take their headline from the page instead of a title made from the slug.
- WHO is read from its news API and CNN from its news sitemap: both feeds were frozen, and WHO now gives 30 items of the week to Health.
- A source can name its own sections. Smithsonian's Environment candidates had come from a guessed /food/ page.
- The Optimist Daily is added, and Good News Network and Reasons to be Cheerful now cover Health.

Rules you may want to change:
- the 0.05 group-fit line
- the 0.02 margin for the five categories
- the phrase lists for non-articles
- The Optimist Daily's reliability rating of 0.7, and not marking it positive_editorial, because it also publishes lifestyle tips

Bugs found on the way:
- Since 4 Oct the trafilatura discovery step had never run: it re-read the dead feed. Run for real, it did worse than section pages, so it is now the last step.
- The labelling batch had neither the mission screen nor the 30-day age limit. That is how today's batch got radio-award nominees, an August phone tip and a NASA media notice. It now has both.
- Claims were cut at abbreviations ("Oct.", "p.m.") and taken from headings. A claim must now be a sentence that ends: claims without end punctuation were labelled 1 time in 6, against 79 in 116 for whole sentences.
- /healthz said the LLM was down for "llama3.1" while Ollama served "llama3.1:latest".

Topic in the rating (added later the same day). Picking Environment over a Culture round and pressing Refresh brought Culture again: Refresh repeats the shown round's topics. Picking other topics now starts a new search for them. The ranking also has a fourth part, "topic" (15%): how well a story fits the topics picked. Over ten labelled rounds, stories of another group in the first twenties went 26 -> 15 with no loss of good stories of the chosen group; 25% would give 9 but lose 5 good ones. Live, an Environment round's first twenty were all Environment but one. The cost: topical hard news (pollution, climate politics) rises a little.
